# Methods for leagues up to 40

## Two-round oil patterns

Keep the first-round weekly rows fixed, including lane and position. If its greedy clash-free runs number g, its last run has length t, there are r more weeks, and L = ⌊(v−1)/(k−1)⌋, any extension needs at least g−1+⌈(t+r)/L⌉ patterns. For a fixed order, taking the longest possible first run is optimal by induction, since cutting it earlier cannot extend any later boundary beyond the greedy one.

For 24 bowlers in fours with the main round first, a 15-pattern solution would need to add two more weeks to the final five-week run. Only 60 quartets avoid every pair already used in those five weeks. An exact cover assigns six quartets to each new week, requiring each player once that week and prohibiting every pair used in the other week. There are zero two-week completions. The verified 16-pattern draw attains the improved lower bound. For 32 in fours the main and oil tails each contain eight weeks. In either case just seven quartets remain eligible. A new week needs eight, so the first round forces a cut at the boundary. The second round needs at least four further patterns, giving eight overall.

For trios, form the conflict graph of the candidate second round, connecting weeks sharing an opponent pair. Enumerate its three-colourings. Try a short colour class first and map its players into the fixed tail so that no edge collides. The 30-bowler oil schedule uses a two-week class to extend its six-week tail. The 36-bowler main schedule uses an eight-week class to extend its three-week tail. A global permutation of the second-round lanes separates each player's two less-frequent lanes. For positions, assign each player a second-round short position different from the first. Successive bipartite matchings then assign one player per group to each position with the required totals. The verifier checks each half separately and also checks the combined totals.

## A four-pattern round for 36 in fours

Label players by four copies of the nine-element field GF(3)[i], with i²=−1. The three Hamilton cycles of the four layer labels supply the first three patterns. Each base week contains the four layer zeros. For each directed cycle edge place the pair ±1 in its first layer with ±(1+i) in its second, and ±i with ±(2+i). Translate all field coordinates together to obtain nine weeks.

Within a layer the four antipodal pairs represent all four directions. On a cycle edge the two groups generate all eight nonzero cross-layer differences. Every layer edge belongs to two Hamilton cycles. After the three patterns, equal-coordinate cross-layer couples have met three times and unequal-coordinate couples twice. The last eight weeks use nonzero slopes a and groups whose layer coordinates are h+sⱼa, for sⱼ equal to 0, 1, 2 and i. This supplies exactly the missing meetings. The expanded schedule verifies the lane assignments and every prefix. The 11-week cap on a clash-free pattern proves that four is optimal.

## Qualifying movements

A lane word records one bowler's lane pair at each block. The published bowling convention follows the cycle of odd positions then even positions. For example, with four bowlers the cycle is 1, 3, 2, 4. Check directed follows after placing the bowlers in their final order.

* **Count opponents.** The maximum meeting count is at least ⌈B(K−1)/(MK−1)⌉. At B>0 the maximum directed follow count is at least one. Hitting both proves optimality for the two requested objectives.
* **Use directed triples.** A trio in cyclic order contributes three directed arcs. Using every arc at most once simultaneously caps meetings at two and follows at one. Generate weeks in symmetry orbits, discard repeated arcs, then combine disjoint arc sets while checking each player's lane counts. In the 12-player, 11-block draw all 132 directed arcs occur exactly once.
* **Combine shorter draws.** A six-block, fully balanced 18-player draw can be doubled with reversed follow cycles. For 15 players, combine weeks of a Kirkman system and their reversals, fitting the lane assignments jointly. For 16 players, two independently relabelled portions of the affine plane of order four cap meetings at two. Match first- and second-half player lane counts, then select one cyclic order per group while prohibiting repeated directed arcs.
* **Reuse one-meeting rounds.** A clash-free subsequence of an existing round gives the primary optimum whenever the selected weeks have the required lane totals. For example, the 40-player draw uses twelve weeks of the published one-meeting fours round. For 18 and 24, another route selects weeks of a nearly Kirkman triple system and refits the lanes. An eight-week, 18-player lane assignment was found by covering the group instances with six disjoint candidate lanes. For 24 players a cyclic lane assignment works for both ten and eleven blocks.
* **Extend finite-field draws.** For M=8 or 9, split players into K layers and use coordinates ℓ+sⱼt in week t on lane ℓ, with distinct nonzero field slopes. After M weeks every player has visited all lanes once and met every player in the other layers once. For 32 players add one internal week in each layer of eight. For 27 players use up to three internal parallel classes in each affine plane of order three. Rotate disjoint sets of lane labels among the layers so a player uses a different extra lane every week.
* **Reorder groups in place.** For larger groups enumerate cyclic orders modulo rotation. Reassign orders to reduce the count of repeated directed arcs while preserving the published groups and lane assignments. This closes both 35-player follow gaps at 11 and 12 blocks.

## Qualifying lower bounds beyond opponent counting

For 6×4 at six blocks, 24 lane words would have to be permutations of six symbols with pairwise Hamming distance at least five to achieve one meeting. Such a permutation array has at most 18 words. For 6×5 at four, five or six blocks, project onto any four columns. Every two columns would contain all 30 off-diagonal ordered pairs. Adjoin six constant rows. The resulting orthogonal array would give two orthogonal Latin squares of order six, which do not exist. [Permutation-array bound, Example 6](https://cse.hkust.edu.hk/faculty/cding/JOURNALS/it021.pdf) · [Stinson's Latin-square proof](https://www.math.uci.edu/~brusso/%5B40%5Dstinson1984.pdf)

For 5×3 at seven blocks, one meeting per couple would give a resolved Kirkman triple system of order 15. For each of its seven isomorphism classes, enumerate all ways for a lane to take one group from each week and visit all players once or twice. No five such lanes partition the weeks. The archive includes the representatives and independent enumeration. For 3×3 at three blocks there are only six possible permutation words and nine players. Two players therefore share all three blocks. In a trio they follow one another in one direction or the other each time, forcing a repeated direction. [Classification of resolved systems](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X2300002X)

For 3×4 at seven blocks, take any four players sharing a group. Each of the other six blocks puts at least two of them on the same lane pair. If meetings were capped at two, these six forced overlaps would exhaust the six couples of the original group. Every couple that met would therefore meet exactly twice. Each player has 21 opponent slots, an odd number. Three meetings are necessary and the published draw achieves that bound with one directed follow.

For 3×4 at eleven blocks, a cap of three would fill each player's 33 opponent slots with exactly three meetings against each of eleven opponents. Balanced lanes would give a GBTD(4,3), ruled out by the block-size-four existence theorem. The published upper bound of four meetings is therefore exact. [Chee, Kiah and Wang](https://doi.org/10.37236/2717)

## The 36-player three-pattern trios round

Build three copies of an eleven-week, 12-player design, split 4+3+4. Put the three-week part first. Each twelve-week transversal system puts one player from each layer in every group and covers each cross-layer couple once. Use two such systems, one alongside each four-week internal part. This gives 3+16+16 weeks. The displayed one-round schedule uses distinct player relabellings within the transversal layers and lane permutations in one week of each system to bring every player's totals to two or three visits per lane. The subsequent mixed-layer search and its remaining constraints are in [START_HERE.md](START_HERE.md).
