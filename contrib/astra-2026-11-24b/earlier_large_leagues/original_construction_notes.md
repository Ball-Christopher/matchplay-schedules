# Constructions and proof details

## One meeting per couple in fours

For 52, 76, 100, 124 and 148 bowlers, use four layers of the cyclic group of odd order q = 13, 19, 25, 31 or 37. Develop one base week by adding each possible offset to all coordinates. Its first quartet takes the zero of each layer. Each other quartet takes the pair ±u from one layer and ±v from another. Using every nonzero direction once in each layer covers all within-layer couples because doubling is invertible. Between the two layers the quartet covers the four differences ±u±v. An exact cover matches the layer directions while reserving specified differences for the final weeks.

Each final week uses a slope a and the q quartets with coordinates h+sⱼa in layers j, as h varies. The JSON records the slopes, base groups and lane labels. In these base-group arrays point IDs start at zero. The expanded weekly draws use IDs starting at one. The lane of a base quartet translates with its offset. In a slope week the lane equals h plus a week-specific offset. Searching only the permutation of base lanes and the slope-week offsets sufficed to put every player on every lane once or twice.

For 64 bowlers use four copies of GF(16). Sixteen transversal weeks initially give player j in the quartet on lane ℓ the coordinate ℓ+(j+1)a in week a, for four distinct nonzero coefficients. Distinct layers meet exactly once. The five parallel classes of the affine plane of order four in each layer cover the remaining couples. With the original transversal lanes each player already visited every lane exactly once. A layer then needs 20 lane labels for its internal lines because lines in distinct directions intersect, and only 16 labels exist. Change the lane permutation in week zero. If lane ℓ now hosts π(ℓ), the point ℓ has lost one visit there and π(ℓ) has gained one. Match an internal line to lane ℓ when it contains ℓ and avoids π(ℓ). Across the five internal weeks require each lane to serve each layer once or twice. This repairs all deficits and avoids all excesses.

For the construction on four layers of Z₃ₘ with four extra players, the long orbit has 3m weeks, the short orbit m weeks, and there is one fixed week. The fixed week puts the four extra players together and groups the four equal finite coordinates. In the short base week each extra player joins the three elements of the order-three subgroup in their own layer. The other quartets are transversals. In the long base week each extra player joins one point in each of the other layers. The remaining quartets pair opposite coordinates between two layers. Exact cover again assigns the unused differences. Splitting the long orbit into three m-week orbits permits a lane action with three m-cycles and one fixed lane. The certificate records the resulting weeks explicitly. This construction uses m=11 for 136 players and m=7 for the 88-player search.

In each one-meeting witness, balanced bowling positions follow by splitting each player's appearances into chronological chunks of four and properly edge-colouring the bipartite graph of chunks versus quartet instances. The colours specify positions. A complete chunk uses all four colours.

## Four patterns for 36 in fours

Use GF(9) = GF(3)[i] with i² = −1. There are four layers. In each of the three undirected Hamilton cycles of the four layer labels, choose an orientation. Start a week with the quartet of zeros. For each oriented edge from u to v add two quartets. The first takes ±1 in u and ±(1+i) in v. The second takes ±i in u and ±(2+i) in v. Translating all coordinates by h gives nine weeks.

In a layer, the four antipodal pairs represent the four directions, so each within-layer couple occurs once per nine-week pattern. Across an edge the eight differences in the two quartets exhaust the nonzero field elements. Opposite layers in the cycle still meet at equal coordinates through the zero quartet. Every layer edge occurs in two cycles. Thus cross-layer couples at equal coordinates have met three times after 27 weeks and those at unequal coordinates twice. Use eight final transversal weeks, one for each nonzero slope a. The coordinates in each quartet are h+sⱼa with four distinct coefficients 0, 1, 2, i. This gives every unequal-coordinate couple its third meeting.

Translate the first three base lane assignments with the points. In the final weeks use lane h plus a fitted offset. Checking the expanded schedule gives 3–4 visits per lane, a maximum prefix meeting spread of 2, and position spread at most 1 at every prefix. A clash-free pattern lasts at most ⌊35/3⌋ = 11 weeks, so 35 weeks require at least four patterns.

## Fixed-first-round extensions

Each published first-round weekly row is copied in lane and position order. In a round of v−1 weeks each player has one less frequent lane and one less frequent position. Match second-round player labels to first-round labels so both less frequent assignments differ. This guarantees balance separately in each half and over the full schedule. Reordering whole weeks in the second round preserves those totals. To join a second-round block to the fixed first-round tail, minimise the number of common opponent edges under the player permutation. The 30-player main result uses such a join.

For a fixed first round with g greedy clash-free runs, a last run of t weeks and r further weeks, the lower bound is g−1+⌈(t+r)/L⌉, where L=⌊(v−1)/(k−1)⌋. The earliest g−1 boundaries cannot be postponed past their greedy positions, and each remaining run has at most L weeks. Greedy cuts also give the exact minimum number of blocks for any supplied complete ordering. The results table gives the lower bound over second rounds and the attained count.

## Qualifying movements

* **4×3, six blocks.** Each bowler has twelve opponent slots and eleven possible opponents. A repeated meeting is unavoidable.
* **6×4, six blocks.** Each of the 24 lane words must be a permutation of six symbols. A cap of one meeting forces any two words to differ in at least five positions. A permutation array of length six and minimum distance five has at most 18 words. See [Constructions of permutation arrays, Example 6](https://cse.hkust.edu.hk/faculty/cding/JOURNALS/it021.pdf).
* **6×5, four through six blocks.** Take any four columns of the lane words. No player repeats a lane. Under a one-meeting cap, each pair of columns contains 30 distinct ordered pairs, hence all 30 off-diagonal pairs. Append the six constant rows. Each pair of columns now contains every ordered pair once. Indexing by the first two columns, the last two give orthogonal Latin squares of order six, contrary to Tarry's theorem. See [Stinson's proof](https://www.math.uci.edu/~brusso/%5B40%5Dstinson1984.pdf).
* **5×3, seven blocks.** A cap of one would use every one of the 105 couples and hence produce a Kirkman triple system of order 15. There are seven isomorphism classes of resolved systems. For each representative enumerate every choice of one group per week that visits each person once or twice. Five disjoint choices must cover the 35 group instances for a lane allocation. None does. The six-block deletion check covers only designs that extend to one of those systems. [Classification of the seven solutions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X2300002X).
* **3×3, three blocks.** Each player has one of six permutations of the three lanes. Nine players force two identical lane words, hence three meetings. In a trio every unordered couple follows in one of the two directions. A couple meeting three times must repeat one direction, so the secondary optimum is at least two. The published draw attains two.

The qualifying JSON draws are the published witnesses. We independently recounted lane visits, meetings and directed follows using the odd-positions-then-even-positions lane cycle. A maximum follow count of one meets the universal lower bound whenever games are played.
