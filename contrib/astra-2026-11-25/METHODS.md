# Constructions and finite proofs

## 24 bowlers in trios

Each of the two rounds has 23 weeks. Its 276 pairs of bowlers must each meet twice, and every player must visit each lane pair two or three times and use each position seven or eight times. Over both rounds the visit counts are five or six per lane pair and fifteen or sixteen per position.

**Lower bound.** Each clash-free week uses two new opponents per player. A pattern can therefore last at most eleven weeks. Greedy cutting gives the fewest patterns for a fixed order: taking the longest possible first run can only shorten the suffix still to be covered. If the fixed round has g greedy runs and a tail of length t, at least g−1 + ceil((t+23)/11) patterns are necessary. The oil first round has g=3 and t=6, giving five. The main first round has g=16 and t=5, giving eighteen.

**Opponent construction.** Write the second round as two, ten and eleven clash-free weeks, called X, Y and Z. A nearly Kirkman triple system on 24 bowlers supplies Z. Its 11 weeks meet all pairs except a perfect matching F. If X contains F, the required missing pairs of the ten-week block Y are exactly the edges of X outside F. These form a cubic graph H. This separates finding the ten-week packing from finding the eleven-week packing.

We constructed Y with an order-three symmetry on eight triples of players. Of its ten weeks, one is fixed by the symmetry and three groups of three weeks are generated by rotation. We retained clash-free packings and recovered X from their missing pairs. In a union of two clash-free weeks, every triangle comes from a single trio: two edges of a triangle must lie in the same week's partition, placing all three vertices in that trio. Removing the twelve matching edges destroys exactly twelve of the sixteen triangles in X, leaving four vertex-disjoint triangles. The other twelve trios become paths of length two. To reconstruct X, choose the centre of each path, restore an edge between its endpoints and check that the restored edges form a perfect matching. Two-colouring the graph of intersecting trios then gives the two opening weeks. The retained solution admits player relabellings into both published first-round tails.

**Eleven weeks compatible with the lane symmetry.** We built Z by doubling twelve points. Start with an order-three invariant, eleven-week design on twelve points in which every pair meets twice. Give each point two copies. Lift each trio to two complementary trios by choosing a binary label for each of its three points. For a pair of underlying points, the two meetings must have opposite relative binary labels. These are linear equations over the two-element field; tying labels across the symmetry orbits makes the lifted system invariant. Thus all pairs meet once except the twelve pairs of copies. An equivariant player relabelling identifies these pairs with F. The completed construction has five fixed weeks and six orbits of three weeks.

**Lane pairs.** Rotate six of the eight lane labels in two cycles of length three and fix the other two. We searched assignments for the fixed lanes, then partitioned the remaining trios between the two lane cycles. Within each cycle we matched partial assignments of phases to the required player visit counts. The actual search input and program are in `construction/`. From that folder, the following regenerates a verified-candidate lane board in the construction's player labels. The first output number is zero on success, followed by the rows.

```sh
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 factored.cpp -o factored
./factored 279 < sym.in > lane-board.txt
```

For each published first round, apply its player relabelling. A global permutation of the second-round lanes then puts each player's two short lane counts on different lanes. Bipartite integral flows assign positions with the analogous short-position condition. The final JSON is the certificate; no assumption about the search algorithm enters the verification.

**What the block lengths mean.** The 2+10+11 construction specifies three clash-free parts of round two. Greedy cutting round two in isolation actually gives 6+6+11. In the complete seasons, the first two weeks of round two join the fixed tail, producing the optimal cuts reported in the results. Both 46-week schedules meet their bounds.

## 15 bowlers qualifying in six blocks

Write a player's six lane visits as a word over five lane labels. Balanced visits require one label twice and each of the other four once. A one-meeting draw is exactly a collection of fifteen such words in which every two words agree in at most one position and each lane occurs three times in every position. Every such collection recovers a valid weekly draw.

**Why 41 prefix cases suffice.** Each player has a unique pair of weeks on which their lane repeats. Give each pair of weeks a weight equal to the number of players repeating on it. The sum of the fifteen weights is fifteen. In the fifteen choices of four weeks, each player's repeated pair is included six times. The average number of repeaters in a four-week selection is therefore six. Pick a selection attaining the minimum.

If that minimum is at most five, the six edge weights on the selected four weeks sum to at most five. Up to permutation of the four weeks, the numbers of profiles at totals zero through five are respectively 1, 1, 3, 6, 11 and 18. If the minimum is six, every four-week sum equals six. For two weeks i and j, compare the four-week sums obtained by adjoining i or j to any three of the other four weeks. Every three of the four differences of incident edge weights sum to zero. Hence all four differences vanish. Varying i and j forces all edge weights equal, and their sum makes them all one. This gives the final profile 111111.

**Exhaustive completion.** Within four weeks, a player either has four distinct symbols or repeats exactly one symbol and uses three distinct symbols. A prefix with fewer distinct symbols cannot cover all five in the last two weeks. The zero profile allows one chosen player to be labelled 0123. Otherwise the code labels a repeated pair as the first two coordinates, and one player repeating on it as 0012. This uses ordinary relabelling of the search instance and retains asymmetric solutions.

For each profile, enumerate the remaining repeated-symbol words on their required coordinate pairs. Fill the other slots from the 120 injective words. Two words sharing any two-coordinate projection are incompatible. Each week–lane cell must ultimately contain three players; the search branches on the cells with fewest spare candidates and enforces that capacity. At every completed four-week prefix, place week five and then exhaust all permitted assignments to week six, enforcing both weekly occupancy and the opponent constraint. Where a player still lacks a symbol at week six its assignment is forced.

The census tested 1,774,642 completed four-week prefixes across the 41 normalized cases. These are search cases, and relabellings can represent the same partial design more than once. No case extends to six weeks. No symmetry of a solution or Kirkman-system embeddability was assumed. The columns of `qualifying15/census.txt` give the profile, number of normalized repeated-word families reaching the injective-word search, number of full prefixes, and whether a completion was found.

**Attainment.** Direct recount of the published witness gives maximum two meetings and one directed follow. Each bowler visits each lane pair once or twice. Since at least one follow occurs in a nonempty draw, both maxima are optimal. The proof concerns these two maxima under the lane-balance requirement.

## Seven weeks of fours for 24 bowlers

The published eight-week hybrid contains seven clash-free fours weeks. Each bowler meets 21 opponents in those weeks, leaving two unmet. The missing pairs form eight disjoint triangles.

For each of the 35 choices of three weeks to retain, enumerate all four-person groups using none of the retained pairs. Generate full candidate weeks by picking a group for the least unassigned player, and enumerate sets of four candidate weeks with disjoint opponent pairs. This allows arbitrary player groupings in each replacement week. It includes all changes to fewer than four weeks.

Exactly seven distinct alternatives to the original labelled packing occur. Every alternative shares exactly three weeks with the original. Explicit permutations of the 24 player labels send the original packing to each alternative, ignoring the order of weeks and groups. Thus every packing reachable by any finite sequence of trades changing at most four weeks per step is isomorphic to the original, by induction. Its eight missing triangles persist. This is a result about the component of this particular published packing. It leaves other seven-week packings open.

The independent Python checker uses integer bitsets for candidate-week compatibility. It verifies the seven player permutations on the groups themselves and recounts the missing triangles.
